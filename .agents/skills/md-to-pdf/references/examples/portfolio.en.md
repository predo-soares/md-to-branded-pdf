# Sample Product Portfolio

Two synthetic case studies illustrate the portfolio structure. They do not describe real clients or outcomes.

## Case Study A: Onboarding Flow

### Context

A fictional product team wanted to understand where new users hesitated during setup. The source brief defined a small study and a limited review window. The team had an existing help guide but no shared map of the decisions a first-time user faced.

![Synthetic onboarding research stages](figure.svg)
*Figure 1. A sample research sequence used only for layout testing.*

### Approach

The sample team mapped the first-run path, observed task attempts, and grouped notes by the point where help was needed. A [research methods reference](https://example.org/methods) was available for comparison. The team kept direct observations separate from interpretations, then reviewed cases that did not match the first pattern.

The review artifact included a task map, a short list of terminology questions, and a record of which notes supported each question. These artifacts let a second reviewer inspect the reasoning without treating the small sample as a measured outcome.

### Outcome

The synthetic exercise produced a list of questions for a later experiment and a draft test scenario. No product improvement metric is claimed, and no product change is presented as completed.

## Case Study B: Documentation Review

### Context

A fictional internal team needed a clearer structure for a technical guide. The existing outline mixed setup steps with background material and used different terms for the same action in two sections. Readers had to switch between pages to complete a short task.

### Approach

The sample team separated task instructions from conceptual explanations, aligned repeated terms, and reviewed the draft with two fictional readers. The review focused on whether each reader could find a starting point and identify the next action. No outcome section is present because the source does not provide supported results.

The case remains deliberately open: a later revision could add results only after the team records a real test and verifies its evidence.
