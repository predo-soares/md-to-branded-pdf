# Sample Project Brief

This synthetic brief shows how a team might plan a small product discovery effort. All names, dates, and values are invented for layout review. The [sample reference](https://example.org) is included to verify a clickable PDF link.

## Purpose and scope

The fictional team wants to understand where a first-time user hesitates while completing a setup task. The brief describes a narrow study, the artifacts it would produce, and decisions that remain open. It makes no claim about a real product or its users.

The initial scope includes a review of existing instructions, five sample task sessions, and a short synthesis meeting. Work outside this scope would be recorded as a follow-up question instead of being presented as completed research.

## Planned activities

1. Review the current task path and write a neutral scenario.
2. Observe sample sessions without changing the instructions between participants.
3. Compare observations with the help guide and record mismatched terms.
4. Present findings with their source notes and confidence limits.

| Activity | Output | Review point |
| --- | --- |
| Task-path review | Annotated steps | Scenario approved |
| Sample sessions | Observation notes | Notes checked |
| Synthesis | Questions and options | Team discussion |

## Decisions to make

- Which task should be used for the first study?
- What evidence would justify testing a new menu label?
- Who will review the raw notes before recommendations are drafted?

> A small synthetic sample can suggest a follow-up test; it cannot establish a general product outcome.
